📖 What is Albion Online?

Albion Online is a sandbox MMORPG developed by Sandbox Interactive. Unlike traditional MMOs, there are no classes — you are what you wear. Your equipped gear determines your abilities, and you can freely switch between playstyles by changing your loadout.

The game features a fully player-driven economy, open-world PvP, territory control, deep crafting, and a rich destiny board progression system. It is available on PC, Mac, iOS, and Android and offers cross-platform play.

📋 Creating Your Account

  • Download the launcher from albiononline.com — available on PC, Mac, Android, and iOS.
  • Create a free account. No credit card required to start.
  • Choose a unique character name — this cannot be changed without a paid name change token.
  • Select a starting appearance. Cosmetics are purely visual and don't affect gameplay.
💡 Premium Note

The game is free, but Premium status (bought with real money or in-game Gold) provides 50% more fame, faster crafting, and increased resource yields. It's worth getting once you understand the game, but not required to start.

🏝️ Tutorial Island — Beginner's Isle

Your first destination is Beginner's Isle, a safe zone that teaches you the fundamentals.

  • 1

    Complete all Tutorial Quests

    The tutorial covers gathering stone/wood, killing mobs, crafting your first gear, and using a crafting station. Don't skip these — they reward silver and gear.

  • 2

    Learn Basic Controls

    Left-click to move, right-click to interact, hotkeys 1-4 for skills. Auto-attack is activated by clicking an enemy. Get comfortable with kiting.

  • 3

    Open the Destiny Board

    Press K to open your Destiny Board. This is your progression map. Understand how Specialization Levels and Learning Points work.

  • 4

    Collect Your Starter Pack Reward

    After completing the tutorial, you receive a starter gear set and some silver to help you in the first Starter City.

  • 5

    Exit to a Starter City

    You'll be transported to one of the five Starter Cities: Thetford, Fort Sterling, Lymhurst, Bridgewatch, or Martlock. Each has its own biome and resource focus.

🌟 The Destiny Board

Albion's classless progression system — unlock everything by using it, not by leveling a fixed class.

📖 How It Works

The Destiny Board (press K) is a massive web of interconnected nodes representing every skill, activity, and equipment type in the game. You unlock nodes by earning Fame — XP earned by performing the activity associated with that node.

There are no arbitrary level gates. Want to use a Tier 8 weapon? Unlock the T8 node by using T4, T5, T6, and T7 versions of that weapon category. Every step of progression is earned through actual gameplay.

🔑 Key Concepts

Specialization Levels (Mastery)

Beyond unlocking a node, you can Specialize deeper into it. Higher specialization = bonus damage, gather yield, craft quality, etc. Max specialization is level 100.

Learning Points

A daily resource (more with Premium) that lets you instantly purchase progress on any Destiny Board node. Use them wisely on your primary focus.

Fame Multipliers

Fame earned scales with content tier. Killing T5 mobs gives more fame than T3 mobs. Use gear one tier above your current unlock for best efficiency.

Respec Costs

Unlike most MMOs, you are never locked in. You can learn everything. There's no penalty for switching — just time. All nodes persist, so breadth is always possible.

💡 Destiny Board Strategy Tips

Focus Early

Pick one weapon type and one armor type and unlock them to T4 before branching out. Spreading fame across too many nodes early slows all of them down.

Best Use of Learning Points

Save Learning Points for nodes that are slow to unlock through gameplay — typically crafting and refining nodes, which require consuming materials. Don't waste them on combat nodes you can farm quickly.

⚠️ Avoid Spreading Thin

A common new-player mistake is trying to unlock everything at once. A character specialized in one path at T6 is far more powerful and profitable than a character with T4 in everything.

🗺️ Where to Gather

💡 Zone Safety vs Reward

Blue zones are safe but resource-depleted. Yellow zones have PvP flags but better resources. Red and Black zones have full-loot PvP and the best resources. Match your gear value to your zone risk.

  • Blue Zones — Safe, T1–T4 resources, no PvP. Good for early gathering.
  • Yellow Zones — Flagged PvP (opt-in), T3–T6 resources. Best starting solo gathering area.
  • Red Zones — Open PvP, T5–T7 resources. High risk, high reward. Use cheap gathering gear.
  • Black Zones — Full-loot PvP, T6–T8 resources. Maximum density and value. Guild required for safety.
  • Roads of Avalon — Instance zones, T6–T8, moderate player danger. Great for solo gatherers.

🎽 Gathering Builds

Use Gatherer's armor — the set bonus gives you extra carrying capacity, reduced weight, and a gathering speed bonus. Never gather in expensive fighting gear in dangerous zones.

Budget Gatherer (T4–T5)

  • T4 Gatherer's Helmet
  • T4 Gatherer's Jacket
  • T4 Gatherer's Workboots
  • T4 Ox or Horse for carrying
  • T4/T5 Tool for the resource type

Advanced Gatherer (T5–T7)

  • T6 Gatherer's Armor (full set)
  • T6 Ox (high carry weight)
  • T6–T7 Tool
  • Pork Omelette or other gathering food
  • Mount with "Flee" ability for escape
⚠️ Don't Over-Stack

In red/black zones, don't fill your inventory completely. Always keep enough movement speed to escape gankers. A half-full inventory is better than dying and losing everything.

🔨 Crafting

Everything in Albion is player-crafted. Master the crafting system to supply the economy and profit.

⚙️ The Crafting Process

  • 1

    Gather or Buy Raw Resources

    Farm resources in the open world, or buy them on the player market. Higher tiers require more materials.

  • 2

    Refine Raw Resources into Refined Materials

    Use the Refinery building in a city. Ore → Metal Bars, Logs → Planks, Fiber → Cloth, etc. Refining also gives Destiny Board fame.

  • 3

    Craft at the Appropriate Station

    Forge for metal items, Alchemist's Lab for potions, Butcher for food, Tailor for cloth, etc. Each station is found in cities and player islands.

  • 4

    Use Focus Points for Better Quality

    Spending Focus (a daily-regenerating resource, more with Premium) on crafts increases the chance of crafting higher-quality items (Normal → Outstanding → Masterpiece).

👕 Armor Types

🧣 Cloth / Robe Armor

Highest damage output, lowest defense. Best for pure casters and mages. The chest piece often provides powerful DPS or healing bonuses. Popular: Mage's Robe, Cleric Robe, Scholar Robe.

🥋 Leather / Jacket Armor

Balanced damage and survival. Popular for rogues, rangers, and hybrid builds. Good energy regeneration bonuses. Popular: Hunter Jacket, Assassin's Jacket.

🛡️ Plate / Armor

Highest defense, reduced damage. Best for tanks and frontline fighters. Offers strong CC and resistance bonuses. Popular: Soldier Armor, Knight Armor, Guardian Helmet.

🎭 Mixing Armor

You can freely mix armor types! Many competitive builds use two different armor types to get specific skill combinations — e.g., a Plate chest for survivability with Leather boots for mobility.

🎯 Popular Meta Builds (2024)

💡 Solo PvE Build

Weapon: Great Arcane Staff or Crossbow | Helmet: Scholar Cowl | Chest: Scholar Robe | Boots: Scholar Sandals. This is the classic "Hellgate" and solo dungeon setup — high damage with self-sustain via the robe's regeneration.

⚔️ Small Group PvP Build

Weapon: Cursed Staff or Spear | Helmet: Mage Cowl | Chest: Assassin's Jacket | Boots: Assassin's Shoes. Burst damage, high mobility, and good escape tools for small-scale fights.

🛡️ Zerg / Large-Scale PvP

Weapon: Hammer / Shield | Helmet: Guardian Helmet | Chest: Knight Armor | Boots: Knight Boots. Frontline tank/CC. Your job is to lock down enemies for your DPS teammates.

🏚️ Random Dungeons Tips

  • Look for glowing portals in open-world zones. They appear on your minimap.
  • Solo dungeons (1 entrance) are generally safer — groups can't follow you in.
  • Group dungeons (3 entrances) are more dangerous but yield much better loot.

⚔️ Flagging System (Yellow Zones)

In yellow zones, you are safe by default. To attack others, you must flag as hostile (click the skull icon). Flagged players are visible to everyone. Killing an innocent player gives you Infamy which can be reduced over time or by dying.

⚠️ Infamy & Karma

In yellow zones, attacking or killing unflagged players builds negative Karma. At high negative Karma, guards will attack you in safe zones. This is reset by dying or waiting over time.

🛡️ Surviving in Red/Black Zones

  • Never bring more than you can afford to lose. The golden rule of Albion.
  • Use a cheap escape build for gathering — Invisibility potions and mounts with "Flee" ability.
  • Watch the minimap constantly for name tags.
  • Travel with a group when possible in black zones.
  • Know your escape route before entering — check the map for zone exits.
  • Use Rations food to boost your mount's movement speed temporarily.
  • If you see a gank group, stop and log off or use a portal to escape.

🗡️ PvP Roles

Ganker / Assassin

Solo or small group predator. Uses high burst damage and speed to catch unaware gatherers or travelers. Typical build: Claws + Light Armor + Stalker Hood.

🔄 Transport Trade (Arbitrage)

Different cities have different supply and demand. You can buy cheap in one city and sell high in another. This is called transport trading and can yield 30–100% profit margins, but requires traveling through dangerous zones.

  • Use albiononline2r.com or community tools to check price differences between cities
  • Use a Mule/Ox mount for maximum carry capacity
  • Hire escorts or travel in groups through dangerous zones
  • Focus on items with high value/weight ratio (refined materials, potions)
⚠️ Transport Risk

Transport traders are prime gank targets. Carrying millions of silver worth of goods through red/black zones is extremely risky. Always weigh the risk vs the profit margin before committing to a trade run.

📈 Market Flipping

Buy low, sell high on the player market. This works because:

  • Players sell resources quickly (often below market rate) to get liquid silver fast
  • Items have different prices in different cities
  • Patch cycles create temporary price spikes when new content releases
💡 Flipping Tip

Focus on refined materials at T5–T6 level. These have consistent demand and the price gaps between buy and sell orders are predictable. Start with 500k silver capital and flip in the same city before attempting cross-city arbitrage.

💡 Tips & Tricks

Hard-earned wisdom from veteran players — mistakes to avoid and shortcuts to success.

🌱 Beginner Tips

  • Complete the Adventurer's Challenge every day — it's the fastest early source of Tomes of Insight (fame books) and rewards.
  • Don't die unnecessarily. Every death costs repair fees and potentially your gear. In red/black zones, this adds up quickly.
  • Learn the market. Check prices before crafting or selling. Some items sell for 10x more in one city than another.
  • Use Tomes of Insight wisely. Save them for crafting/refining nodes, not combat nodes you can farm quickly.
  • Join a guild early. Solo Albion is hard. A good guild provides safety, shared resources, and knowledge.
  • Read tooltips. Every ability has detailed tooltip text. Understanding your skills is the biggest early advantage.

💰 Economy Tips

  • Sell refined materials, not raw resources. Refining adds significant value and the fame helps your Destiny Board.
  • Use buy orders. Placing buy orders is almost always cheaper than instant buying. Be patient.
  • Check Caerleon prices. The central black zone city usually has the highest sell prices for most items.
  • Never craft with Focus points on T4. Focus is most valuable at T6+ where quality differences command premium prices.
  • Farm during off-peak hours. Resource nodes are less contested at night in your region's timezone.

⚔️ Combat Tips

  • Never stand still. Constant movement makes you harder to CC and skill-shot abilities miss more often.
  • Learn ability ranges. Many spells require specific range to cast. Play your positioning for your weapon's optimal range.
  • Food matters. Always eat buff food before PvP. It can be the difference between winning and losing close fights.
  • Know your escape tools. Every build should have at minimum one speed boost or blink ability before going into red/black zones.
  • Counter your counters. Research what builds beat yours. Hard counters exist. Build accordingly and don't take unfavorable fights.

🔧 Quality of Life Tips

  • Use Guild/Alliance chat to call out gankers in your zone — a heads up to guildmates is worth its weight in silver.
  • Set in-game alarms for farming plots on your island so you never miss a harvest cycle.
  • Keybind your mount — getting on/off quickly during PvP can save your life.
  • Use the third-party tool albiononline2r.com or Albion Online market tools to track item prices.
  • Keep a secondary "travel build" — cheap gear you don't mind losing for daily travel between cities.
  • Store backup gear in multiple cities. Losing gear in a city you're not in doesn't have to stop your day.

❌ Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Mistake: Bringing Expensive Gear to New Zones

Never go into a new zone (especially red/black) for the first time with your best gear. Scout it in cheap gear first to understand the danger level and common gank spots.

❌ Mistake: Spreading the Destiny Board Thin

Trying to unlock T4 in every weapon and armor type before specializing means you spend months at mediocre gear. Pick one path and push it to T6 first.

❌ Mistake: Not Using Premium When Affordable

The +50% fame bonus from Premium compounds massively over time. Once you can afford it (via Silver/Gold exchange), Premium is almost always worth it.

❌ Mistake: Ignoring the Economy

Many players who quit Albion do so because they "can't afford gear." The economy is learnable. Understand crafting returns, market prices, and transport routes — gear stops being a problem entirely.

🌟 Final Tip

Albion Online rewards patience and learning above all else. The players who succeed long-term are those who understand the economy, build around efficient progression, and find a community they enjoy playing with. Your first month will be the hardest — push through it and the game opens up enormously.

A clear week-by-week path from complete beginner to endgame. Follow this and you won't get lost.

💡 How to Use This Guide

Don't try to rush every stage. Each milestone builds on the last. If you feel stuck, you're probably trying to skip a step — go back one stage and make sure you've completed those goals first.

🌱 Stage 1 — Day 1 to 3: The Foundation

Your only goal right now is learning how the game works. Don't worry about efficiency yet.

  • 1

    Complete Beginner's Isle fully

    Do every tutorial quest. You'll earn free gear, silver, and Tomes of Insight. Don't skip or rush — these rewards are free fame you can't get back.

  • 2

    Open the Destiny Board (K) and pick ONE weapon

    Don't pick two. Swords, Axes, Bows, Fire Staff — anything. You'll branch out later. Trying to unlock everything simultaneously is the #1 reason new players quit feeling like they made no progress.

  • 3

    Choose a Starter City and sell everything you don't need

    Check the market for your raw resources. Always sell on the market (sell order) rather than to the vendor — you'll earn 3–10× more silver.

  • 4

    Start the Adventurer's Challenge

    Press the chest icon on your HUD. Complete daily tasks for Tomes of Insight and premium rewards. This is free daily progress — do it every single day.

✅ Stage 1 Exit Goal

T3 weapon unlocked on Destiny Board, 50,000+ silver in your bank, understand how the market works.

⚒️ Stage 2 — Week 1: Reaching T4

T4 is the first real gear tier. Everything before this is just the tutorial. This is where the game actually starts.

  • 1

    Farm Blue Zone mobs around your Starter City

    Kill T3–T4 mobs consistently. Use T3 gear you craft or buy cheaply. The goal is 9,000 fame on your chosen weapon to unlock T4.

  • 2

    Run Solo Expeditions from the Expedition Master NPC

    These are 100% safe instanced dungeons. Do T3 and T4 expeditions daily — they give good fame and guaranteed silver drops. Free daily run available each day.

  • 3

    Gather T3–T4 resources in Blue/Yellow zones and refine them

    Gathering gives both silver AND gathering fame on your Destiny Board simultaneously. Pick one resource type (ore, wood, fibre, hide, or stone) and focus on it.

  • 4

    Craft and sell T3–T4 gear on the market

    Don't just sell raw materials. Refined bars, planks, and cloth sell for 20–50% more than raw resources. Simple crafting is almost always worth it at this stage.

  • 5

    Use Tomes of Insight on crafting and gathering nodes

    Save your Tomes for nodes that are slow to grind (refining, crafting). Combat nodes fill up fast through normal play. Don't waste Tomes on sword/bow fame you'll naturally earn anyway.

✅ Stage 2 Exit Goal

T4 weapon and armour unlocked. 200,000+ silver saved. Understand refining vs raw selling.

⚔️ Stage 3 — Weeks 2–3: T4 to T5, Join a Guild

This is where most new players stall. Yellow zones feel risky but they're essential. Push through them.

  • 1

    Enter Yellow Zones with T4 gear

    Yellow zones have flagged PvP (you have to opt in to attack). You can gather and kill mobs safely as long as you don't flag yourself. T5 resources and better dungeon portals start here.

  • 2

    Farm Yellow Zone Randomised Dungeons

    Look for glowing portals in yellow zones. Solo dungeons (one entrance) are the safest. T4–T5 dungeons give 5,000–20,000 fame per run and solid silver drops. This is your primary fame source now.

  • 3

    Join a beginner-friendly guild

    Check the in-game Guild Finder or post in General chat. A guild gives you safety in numbers, shared knowledge, and access to a guild island with better crafting stations. Don't solo the whole game.

  • 4

    Buy Premium with Silver if you can afford it

    Once you're making 300,000+ silver/day, check the Gold Market. Buy Gold with Silver and activate Premium. The +50% fame bonus means every hour of grinding from this point forward is 50% more effective. It compounds enormously.

✅ Stage 3 Exit Goal

T5 weapon unlocked. In a guild. Premium active. 1M+ silver saved. Comfortable in yellow zones.

🔴 Stage 4 — Month 1: T5 to T6, First Red Zone

Red zones are scary at first. They're also where real money starts. Understand the risk before you go in — don't go in blind.

  • 1

    Scout red zones in cheap gear first

    Your first few visits to red zones should be in T4 throw-away gear. Learn the map, find the dungeon portal locations, identify the gank corridors. Knowledge is worth more than any gear piece.

  • 2

    Farm Red Zone Solo Dungeons in T5 gear

    Red zone solo dungeons give 2–3× the fame and silver of yellow zone equivalents. T5 dungeons: ~20,000–50,000 fame per run. T6 dungeons: ~50,000–100,000 fame per run. This is your new primary grind.

  • 3

    Buy a Personal Island (requires Premium)

    Visit the Island Merchant NPC in any Starter City. A Level 1 island costs around 12,500 silver to rent. Place farming plots immediately — passive income starts the moment your crops go in the ground.

  • 4

    Specialise your Destiny Board path deeper

    By now you should have your main weapon at T5. Push it to T6 before branching. Start speccing into the matching armour type (cloth for mage, leather for ranged, plate for melee tank).

✅ Stage 4 Exit Goal

T6 weapon unlocked. Personal Island Level 2+ with farming running. 5M+ silver saved. Making 500k–1M/day.

⚫ Stage 5 — Months 2–3: T6 to T7, Black Zone Access

Black zones are full-loot PvP. Go with your guild. Keep your gear value reasonable. The rewards justify the risk.

  • 1

    Travel to black zones with guildmates

    Never go alone your first time. Travel in a group of 3–5 through the black zone portal. Learn the safe paths and the high-traffic gank corridors before going solo.

  • 2

    Farm Black Zone Solo Dungeons (T6–T7)

    These give 100,000–500,000 fame per run and 500k–2M+ silver. This is when your income jumps dramatically. Keep gear value reasonable — T6 normal quality, not T7 enchanted.

  • 3

    Explore the Roads of Avalon

    Roads portals appear in yellow/red/black zones. The Roads have excellent T7–T8 resource density with fewer players than black zones. Great for solo gathering with a fast escape mount.

  • 4

    Upgrade your island to Level 4–5 and start mount breeding

    By this stage your island should be generating meaningful passive income. Add Animal Pastures and start breeding T5 horses and oxen. See the Island Guide for full details.

✅ Stage 5 Exit Goal

T7 weapon. Island Level 4+ with mount breeding running. Making 2M–5M/day. Comfortable in black zones.

👑 Stage 6 — Endgame: T7 to T8 and Beyond

You're now in the top tier of the game. The grind to T8 is long but you have consistent income to fund it.

  • 1

    Push weapon to T8 through Black Zone content

    T8 requires 4,500,000 fame. At 500k fame/hour in black zones with Premium, that's 9 hours of focused grinding. Most players reach T8 over 2–4 weeks of consistent play.

  • 2

    Join a guild running Hardcore Expeditions (HCE)

    HCE is the best fame/silver PvE content in the game. It requires T8 gear and a coordinated 5-man team. Find HCE groups in your guild or through LFG channels.

  • 3

    Participate in Crystal League and Guild Season

    Crystal League 5v5 fights earn Season Points toward guild ranking. Top guilds at season end receive exclusive mounts, cosmetics, and prestige. This is the competitive endgame.

  • 4

    Diversify your income streams

    At endgame, the best players have 3–4 income sources running simultaneously: HCE, island passive, crafting with focus, and black zone gathering. No single source dominates — redundancy is stability.

✅ Endgame Goal

T8 weapon. Multiple income streams generating 5M–15M+/day. Active guild with Crystal League participation.

Best Uses for Tomes of Insight

  • Crafting nodes — slow to grind naturally
  • Cooking & Alchemy — material-intensive
  • Never on combat nodes — you'll earn these naturally through play

📌 The Core Rule

At every stage the same principle applies: add value before selling. Raw ore is worth X. Refined bars are worth 1.4X. Crafted gear is worth 2X. Whoever does the least processing earns the least. Move up the value chain as fast as your Destiny Board allows.

🪙 Stage 1 — 0 to 100,000 Silver: Getting Started

You just started. Your only assets are time and the tutorial rewards.

Best Methods:

  • Sell tutorial rewards on the market — don't vendor them. Market prices are almost always higher.
  • Gather T2–T3 resources in blue zones and refine them. Refined T2 materials sell surprisingly well as crafting components.
  • Complete Adventurer's Challenge daily — the Tomes you earn can be sold directly on the market for instant silver.
  • Run T3 expeditions for the silver bag drops. Small but consistent.
Target: 100k silver in 2–3 days of casual play. Don't stress this stage — it passes quickly.

🪙🪙 Stage 2 — 100k to 1M Silver: Finding Your Method

You have T4 gear and some market knowledge. Now pick a lane.

Path A: Gathering + Refining

  • Gather T4 resources in yellow zones (1–2 hours/session)
  • Refine everything — never sell raw at this stage
  • Sell refined materials at the best-priced city
  • Expected: 150,000–300,000 silver/hour
  • Best for: Safe play, no PvP

Path B: Dungeon Farming

  • Run yellow zone solo dungeons with T4–T5 gear
  • Sell all dropped gear and materials
  • Expected: 200,000–400,000 silver/hour
  • Best for: Players who enjoy combat
💡 Key Move at This Stage

Start placing buy orders on the market for resources you need rather than instant-buying. Buy orders are consistently 10–20% cheaper. The patience pays off immediately.

🪙🪙🪙 Stage 3 — 1M to 10M Silver: Scaling Up

Stack These Income Sources Simultaneously:

  • Red zone gathering: T5–T6 resources in red zones earn 400,000–900,000 silver/hour. Use gatherer gear, not fighting gear. Have an escape plan.
  • T5–T6 dungeon runs: 300,000–700,000 silver/hour in red zone solo dungeons with a solid build.
  • Transport trading: Buy cheap in one city, sell expensive in another. Use albiononline2r.com to find price gaps of 20%+. Use an Ox for carry weight.
📊 What 1M–10M Actually Looks Like Per Day

2 hours of red zone gathering = ~1.5M silver. 30 min island upkeep daily = ~70k/day passive. 2 transport runs/day = ~500k–1M. Combined: easily hitting 3–5M/day with 3–4 hours played.

⚒️ Crafting for Profit

The complete crafting system explained — focus points, city bonuses, refining margins, and exactly which items print silver at each tier.

🔑 The Core Insight Most Players Miss

Crafting in Albion isn't just about making things — it's about the resource return rate system. When you craft at a specialised city station using Focus Points, a percentage of the materials you put in come back to you. This means your effective material cost is dramatically lower than buying everything at market rate.

💡 The Maths That Makes Crafting Profitable

Example: You craft 100 T6 swords using 10,000 silver worth of materials. With 47% return rate, you get back ~4,700 silver worth of materials. Your real cost was only 5,300 silver. If each sword sells for 200 silver (200 × 100 = 20,000 silver), your profit is ~14,700 silver. Without return rate, you'd barely break even. The return rate IS the profit.

📊 Understanding Return Rates

Return rate is the % of materials you get back when crafting. It's determined by three things stacked together:

FactorMax BonusHow to Get It
🏙️ City Station BonusUp to +47%Craft at the city specialised for that item type
⭐ Crafting SpecialisationUp to +53%Level your specific crafting node on Destiny Board to 100
🔵 Focus PointsActivates full rateUse Focus when crafting (regenerates 10,000/day with Premium)
⚠️ Focus Points are Essential

Without spending Focus Points, your return rate drops drastically. Always use Focus when crafting for profit. Without Premium you get fewer Focus points per day — another reason Premium pays for itself.

🎯 Picking Your Crafting Specialisation

You cannot be a master crafter of everything — spreading across too many crafting nodes means your return rate is mediocre everywhere. Pick one path and push it deep.

Best Solo Crafting Paths

  • Food (Cook): Lowest barrier to entry, consistent demand, Island farming feeds your supply chain. Best starter craft.
  • Potions (Alchemist): High margin, especially Healing and Resistance potions consumed by PvP players daily.
  • Leather Armour: Moderate competition, steady demand from ranged/rogue builds.

Best High-Volume Paths

  • T6 Weapons (any type): Enormous player base at T6 constantly replacing gear lost in PvP.
  • Cloth Armour (Robes): Mage builds are extremely popular — always demand for scholar robes and cowls.
  • Refining (any material): Least glamorous but incredibly consistent — every crafter needs refined materials.

🧮 How to Check if a Craft is Profitable

  • 1

    Check the sell price of the finished item

    Look at the lowest sell order on the market for the finished item. This is your revenue ceiling — you need to undercut it slightly to sell.

  • 2

    Calculate material cost with return rate

    Look up the buy order price for all required materials. Multiply total material cost by (1 − your return rate). That's your actual cost. Example: 10,000 silver materials × (1 − 0.40) = 6,000 silver real cost.

  • 3

    Subtract the market tax (3–5%)

    The market charges a listing fee plus a transaction fee when items sell. Factor in roughly 6–8% total on the sell price.

  • 4

    If margin > 15%, it's worth doing at volume

    Below 15% margin the time and risk usually isn't worth it unless you're doing massive volume. Above 20% is comfortable. Above 30% is excellent.

💡 Use Community Tools

The site albiononline2r.com and Albion Online Data Project show live market prices across all cities. Always check prices before committing to a crafting batch — margins change as the market shifts.

📋 Crafting as a Service (Charging Fees)

Once you're T6+ specialised, other players will pay you to craft their materials for them — they supply the resources, you supply the specialisation and Focus, and you keep a portion of the returned materials as your fee. This is called crafting for others and requires zero capital investment on your part.

  • Advertise in city chat: "T6 weapon crafting, 50% of returned mats as fee"
  • Standard fee is 40–60% of the returned materials
  • Zero risk — you never own the materials, so you can't lose silver
  • Great way to build crafting fame without buying your own material supply

🗺️ How the World is Connected

Albion's world is a grid of interconnected zones. To travel between cities you pass through a series of zones, each with its own danger level. Understanding the path between your city and your destination is essential before you travel with anything valuable.

🏙️ Royal Road — The Safe Highway

All five Starter Cities are connected by the Royal Road — a series of blue (safe) zones. You can travel between any two Starter Cities entirely through safe zones if you stick to this road. It's slower than shortcuts, but zero risk. Use it when carrying anything you can't afford to lose.

⚠️ Zone Transitions are the Danger Point

Gankers camp the entry portals of zones — especially where a safe zone meets a red or black zone. The few seconds after zoning in are when you're most vulnerable. Always check who is in zone with you immediately after entering.

🌀 Using Roads of Avalon for Travel

The Roads of Avalon aren't just for content — they're also a travel network. Roads portals connect to zones across the entire map, sometimes letting you jump from one side of the world to the other much faster than the surface route.

  • Roads portals appear as glowing swirls in yellow, red, and black zones
  • The exit portal of a Roads zone connects to a different surface zone — check the map before committing
  • Roads have fewer players than black zones making them relatively safer for transit
  • Portal locations shift over time — the Roads network isn't static
  • Your guild's hideout in the Roads can serve as a fast-travel waypoint

👁️ Gank Awareness — Spotting Danger Early

  • Watch your minimap constantly. Player name tags appear before you can see them on screen. If unknown names appear near your position, be alert.
  • Check zone population. The zone HUD shows how many players are in your current zone. 10+ players in a red zone is a red flag — it means a gank group is active.
  • Slow down at portals. Gankers wait at zone transition portals. Before entering, check if anyone is sitting idle at the entry point.
  • Ask in guild chat. If your guild has members in the same region, ask if any zones are hot before traveling through them.
  • Use the in-game map. Press M and check the zone connection layout before planning a route with valuable cargo.

🏃 What to Do When You're Being Chased

  • 1

    Pop your Invisibility Potion immediately

    Don't wait. The moment you spot a ganker closing in, pop Invisibility. The 30 seconds it buys is usually enough to reach a zone portal or break line of sight.

  • 2

    Move toward the nearest zone exit — not away randomly

    Open your map and identify the nearest zone exit. Run directly toward it. Running in random directions wastes time and lets them cut you off.

  • 3

    Dismount if your mount is slow

    Oxen and mules are slower than gankers' horses. If caught on an Ox, dismount and run on foot — you're often faster and more agile than you think.

  • 4

    Use terrain — trees, rocks, buildings break line of sight

    Some abilities require line of sight. Juke around terrain obstacles to interrupt gap-closers and give the invisibility potion time to kick in.

  • 5

    If cornered — fight or log out strategically

    If you have a combat build and they're a solo ganker, sometimes fighting back works. If outnumbered, open your inventory and drop the most valuable items you're carrying into a hidden spot. A partial loss is better than a total loss.

🧳 The Cardinal Rule of Travel

💀 Never Carry More Than You Can Afford to Lose

This applies every single time you leave a safe zone. Before you set out, ask yourself: "If I die right now, will I be okay?" If the answer is no, either take a safer route, get an escort, or reduce what you're carrying. The players who last in Albion are not the ones who never get ganked — they're the ones who never carry more than they can rebuild from.

🌾 Strategy 1 — Crop Selling (Beginner)

The simplest operation. Grow crops, sell them raw on the market. No animals, no complex supply chains — just plant, harvest, sell, repeat.

Best Crops to Sell Raw

  • Wheat / Grain — constant demand from cooks
  • Pumpkin — high value cooking ingredient
  • Cabbage — consistent market demand
  • Yarrow (herb) — alchemy ingredient, good price
  • Burdock (herb) — high demand, reliable price

Setup Steps

  • Level 1 island → place 2 Crop Plots
  • Plant Wheat and Pumpkins to start
  • Harvest every ~22 hours
  • Use Focus Points when tending for +yield
  • Sell on market, not to vendors

🍳 Strategy 2 — Cooking Supply Chain

Grow the ingredients cooks need, or cook the food yourself and sell the finished product. Cooking is the most beginner-accessible production chain because ingredients are cheap and demand is permanent.

Two sub-approaches:

A) Sell Ingredients to Cooks

  • Grow Wheat, Vegetables, Herbs on your island
  • Sell raw on the market
  • Zero processing needed
  • ~300k–700k/week with Level 3 island
  • Easiest setup, lowest margin

B) Cook and Sell Finished Food

  • Buy meat from market + grow vegetables
  • Cook at Butcher/Cook station in city
  • Sell Pork Omelette, Beef Stew, Soup
  • ~500k–1.5M/week with T5+ cooking spec
  • More effort but 2–3× the margin

⚗️ Strategy 3 — Alchemy Supply Chain

Grow herbs on your island and either sell them or craft potions. Healing and Resistance potions have consistent demand from PvP players who consume them constantly.

  • Best herbs to grow: Yarrow, Burdock, Teasel, Foxglove
  • Best potions to craft: Healing Potion (T4–T6), Resistance Potion (T5–T7)
  • Craft at the Alchemist's Lab in any city
  • T5+ Alchemy specialisation + Focus gives ~40% return rate on ingredients
  • Sell in Caerleon or the busiest market you have access to
  • Demand spikes after major PvP content (territory fights, season updates)

🐴 Strategy 4 — Horse & Ox Breeding (Recommended Core Strategy)

The best balance of income, setup complexity, and daily time. See the full breakdown in the Island & Mount Guide for exact animal costs, feed requirements, sell prices, and the daily routine.

💡 Quick Summary

Level 3 island → 2 Animal Pastures → T5 horses or T5–T6 oxen → feed with Hay from your Wheat plots → harvest grown mounts → sell at Caerleon. Earns 1M–4M silver/week passively with 10–12 minutes/day.

⚠️ Exotic Mount Risks

Baby exotic animals can be expensive and sometimes hard to find on the market. Prices fluctuate significantly. Don't commit large capital to exotic breeding until you understand the market cycles for that specific mount in your region.

⚒️ Strategy 6 — Island as a Crafting Hub

Place crafting stations on your island and craft there instead of city stations. You pay no city usage fees, and at high specialisation the difference is significant.

  • City crafting stations charge a usage fee (typically 3–8% of item value)
  • Your island station charges nothing — 100% of the value stays with you
  • Best for high-volume crafters doing 1M+ silver of crafting per day
  • Island stations don't have city return rate bonuses — trade-off to consider
  • Best combined with the cooking or alchemy strategies above for zero-fee food/potion crafting
